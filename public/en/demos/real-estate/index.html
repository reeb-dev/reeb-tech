Places

Get to know the area

Civic Center

The Civic Center opens onto Bariloche’s waterfront and lake. Downtown homes are close to Mitre Street and the shoreline.

Lago Nahuel Huapi

The lake shapes the city’s landscape, with shoreline, mountains and snow-capped peaks. Check each listing for its actual view.

Circuito Chico

A western route through shoreline, forest and mountains. Winter road access is an important detail for every home.

Llao Llao

A western peninsula between lake and forest, known for homes with water or woodland views.

Cerro Catedral

Bariloche’s winter mountain southwest of downtown. Snow and access conditions are part of daily life nearby.

Colonia Suiza

A wooded area west of the city, quieter than downtown during most of the year.

Puerto Pañuelo

A western bay near Llao Llao. Lake views are prominent, while winter access remains an important consideration.

Dina Huapi

A quieter town on the eastern shore, separate from downtown Bariloche.

Moving here

What living here means

These listings are for permanent homes or second residences. They do not promise investment returns or short-term rental income.

The lake is close

Downtown is within walking distance of the waterfront. In western areas, a lake view may be part of the property; each listing states what is actually visible.

Homes built for winter

Snow and cold are normal. Fireplaces, radiators, radiant floors and covered parking matter during the colder months.

A different pace

Melipal, Colonia Suiza and El Bolsón are quieter than Mitre Street in peak season. Downtown keeps shops and services close by.

Permanent or second home

Most listings are intended for year-round living. A second-home option is stated clearly without promises of guaranteed returns.
